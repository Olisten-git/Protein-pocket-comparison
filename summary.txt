==========================================================================================
SUMMARY: BINDING-POCKET COMPARISON, HUMAN (3T0H, 5CF0) vs PARASITE (3K60)
Aim: find pocket differences that can be exploited to design a parasite-selective ligand
==========================================================================================

1. STRUCTURES USED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  3T0H  human, chain A, no ligand ("empty")
  5CF0  human, chain A, ligand FJS (inhibitor)       -> defines the human inhibitor pocket
  3K60  parasite, chain A, ligand ADP                -> defines the parasite pocket

  Note: the two reference ligands are different (FJS inhibitor in human, ADP nucleotide in
  parasite), so the pockets are compared by position in 3D, not by ligand-contact lists alone.


2. METHOD
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Tools: PyMOL + Python scripts (pocket_compare_v2.py, pocket_compare_human.py,
  pocket_compare_3way.py). Outputs: *_report.txt and *_positions.csv for each run.

  Step 1  Superposition. CE alignment (cealign) of chain A protein atoms, with 3T0H as the
          common frame:
            3K60 -> 3T0H : RMSD 1.77 A over 200 residues
            5CF0 -> 3T0H : RMSD 1.09 A over 200 residues
          CE also gives a 1:1 residue pairing (200 pairs in each case).

  Step 2  Pocket definition. Pocket = every residue with ANY atom within 5.0 A of the ligand
          (FJS in 5CF0, ADP in 3K60; ADP and FJS footprints were also transferred onto 3T0H).

  Step 3  Position table. Union of all pocket lists, so cut-off edge effects do not hide
          residues. Each position is labelled by its 3T0H residue number (P1-P34) and
          carries the matching 5CF0 and 3K60 residue.

  Step 4  Register correction. CE mis-paired Gly121 (3K60) with Gly132 (3T0H) in the first
          2-way run. Human 132-138 (GQFGVGF) is the same sequence as 3K60 118-124, so this
          segment was re-paired manually (offset 14). All results below use the corrected pairing.

  Step 5  Per-position comparison:
          - IDENTICAL / SIMILAR (same group: AVILM, FWY, ST, NQ, DE, KRH, G, P, C) / DIFFERENT
          - change in charge, H-bond donors/acceptors, aromaticity, hydrophobicity (Kyte-
            Doolittle) and volume (A^3) going human -> parasite
          - CA-CA and side-chain-centroid distance between structures. A large value on an
            identical residue = the side chain/loop sits somewhere else (flagged SHIFTED).
          - Human flexibility: a position is MOBILE if its side chain moves >1.5 A between
            3T0H and 5CF0, otherwise rigid. (Rigid human positions are safest to exploit.)
          - Ligand reach: closest FJS heavy atom to each residue; contact <=4.5 A,
            edge <=6.0 A (reachable by growing the ligand), far >6.0 A.
          - SHIFTED = same residue but side chain >2.5 A from its 3T0H position in 3K60.

4. WHAT WAS DISCOVERED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  a) The two human structures are essentially the same pocket. Within the 27 positions of
     the 5CF0/3T0H comparison, all 27 are identical in sequence. Only Lys58 and the loop
     Asn106-Ile110 differ in conformation (side chain / CA shifts of ~3-9 A).

  b) The parasite pocket is highly conserved relative to human. Of the 34 positions:
       20  CONSERVED   same residue, same place in human and parasite
        2  DIFFERENT   different residue type (Ser52->Ala, His154->Asn)
        3  SIMILAR     same group, different size/donors (Lys112->Arg, Thr152->Ser, Val186->Ile)
        9  SHIFTED     same residue, but the backbone/side chain sits elsewhere in 3K60
     So only 5 positions differ in sequence, and the rest of the selectivity must come from
     pocket shape (loop positions).

  c) The 20 fully conserved residues are the potency anchors (no selectivity expected):
       Glu47 Leu48 Asn51 Asp54 Ala55 Lys58 Asp93 Gly95 Ile96 Gly97 Met98 Asp102 Leu103
       Leu107 Gly137 Phe138 Tyr139 Val150 Trp162 Thr184   (3T0H numbering)
     The key polar contacts of the site (Asn51, Asp93, Thr184 region) are among them, so a
     ligand built on these contacts will bind both proteins.

  d) Overall pocket chemistry is almost the same (net charge -2 in both on matched positions;
     mean hydrophobicity 0.02 vs 0.02 for the 3K60 comparison), so selectivity will come from
     a few specific residues and shape, not from a global polarity or charge difference.


5. DIFFERENCES THAT CAN BE EXPLOITED  (human -> parasite, 3T0H numbering first)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A. Sequence handles, ranked by usefulness (human position must be rigid and reachable):

   1) SER52 -> ALA38        rigid, CONTACT (FJS 3.9 A)       loses 1 donor + 1 acceptor
      Best handle: in contact with the ligand, rigid in both human structures. Parasite has a
      non-polar Ala here. Idea: avoid depending on an H-bond to Ser52; place a hydrophobic
      group against Ala38 that a polar Ser OH would penalise in human.

   2) THR152 -> SER138      rigid, EDGE (5.1 A)              smaller (-27 A^3)
      Parasite has a little more room (Thr methyl lost). Idea: a small substituent grown
      from the scaffold toward this spot fits the parasite, and may clash with Thr152 in human.

   3) HIS154 -> ASN140      rigid, EDGE (5.9 A)              loses aromatic ring; smaller (-39 A^3)
      Human has an imidazole (possible pi-stacking, His(+/0)), parasite has a smaller
      polar Asn. Idea: grow a group into the extra space / avoid the aromatic partner.

   4) VAL186 -> ILE173      rigid, CONTACT (3.4 A)           bulkier (+27 A^3)
      The parasite has the extra methyl, so this part of the parasite pocket is TIGHTER.
      Do not extend a bulky group here (would favour human); use as a shape constraint,
      with a ligand fragment complementary to Ile173. Modest handle.

   5) LYS112 -> ARG98       rigid, FAR (9.9 A from FJS; 2.4 A from ADP)   +2 donors
      Out of reach of the 5CF0 ligand, but within reach of an ADP-site-sized or extended
      ligand. Arg98 offers more H-bond donors/a larger cationic group. Longer-term idea.

  B. Shape handles (same residue, different position; needs caution, see section 6):
     Human 106-112 (Asn106, Leu107, Gly108, Thr109, Ile110, Ala111) loop: in 3K60 this
     segment adopts its own conformation (CA-CA up to ~9 A from 3T0H), and most of these
     residues are within contact distance of FJS (3.0-4.4 A). Residues Gly108 and Thr109 are
     the largest movers.
     Human 132-137 (Gly132, Phe134, Gly135, Val136): rigid in humans but displaced 4-8 A in
     3K60. Gly135 (rigid, contact 3.9 A) is the most relevant: the parasite wall at this spot
     is in a different place, which changes the pocket outline.
     Design implication: a ligand that fits the human 106-112 conformation may not be
     compatible with the parasite loop, and vice versa.

  C. Mobile human positions (Lys58, Asn106, Leu107, Gly108, Thr109, Ile110, Ala111) are
     a liability for human-targeting designs. Ligands must tolerate them in human; this
     suggests selectivity based on rigid positions (Section A) is more reliable.


6. CAVEATS (for discussion)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - The comparison is based on ONE parasite structure (3K60) that has ADP, not an inhibitor.
    The parasite pocket may rearrange when an inhibitor binds, so SHIFTED loop positions could
    partly reflect ligand state or crystal packing rather than a true, exploitable difference.
  - Only two human structures were used to judge rigid vs mobile.
  - "Reach" distances refer to the human inhibitor FJS; the parasite ligand is ADP, so reach
    in the parasite pocket was not measured with an inhibitor.
  - Volume/hydrophobicity/donor-acceptor changes are simple residue-level scales; they say
    nothing about water networks, protonation (His154) or entropy.
  - Selectivity directions in Section A are structural hypotheses and are not yet tested.
  - The first 2-way run (pocket_compare_v2.py, 3K60 vs 3T0H) contains the Gly121/Gly132
    mis-pairing (positions P21, P22 there) and should be superseded by the 3-way results.


7. SUGGESTED NEXT STEPS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Sequence alignment of the full proteins to confirm the 5 sequence differences and check
     whether neighbouring residues (outside 5 A) also differ.
  2. Dock/score candidate ligands in both pockets, with special attention to Ser52/Ala38,
     Thr152/Ser138 and His154/Asn140.
  3. Run short MD on 3K60 (apo and with an inhibitor-like ligand) to see whether the loop
     conformations seen in the crystal structure are stable.
  4. Look for further parasite and human structures with inhibitors to confirm the loop
     differences.
