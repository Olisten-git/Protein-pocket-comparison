LIGAND DESIGN PLAN
Structure-guided design of selective ligands using differential pocket mapping
==============================================================================================
Structures used : 3K60 (ADP-bound, non-human protein), 3T0H (human, apo), 5CF0 (human, bound to FJS)
Analysis files  : pocket_report.txt, human_report.txt, threeway_report.txt (+ CSV tables)
Target protein  : ______________________   Off-target protein : ______________________

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. AIM
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To design small-molecule ligands that bind the target pocket with high potency and discriminate
against the off-target pocket, by exploiting the few residue differences between the two
proteins while relying on the many conserved residues for affinity.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. BASIS OF THE PLAN: WHAT THE STRUCTURAL COMPARISON SHOWS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I superimposed the structures (CE alignment, 200 residues; RMSD 1.77 A for 3K60 vs 3T0H and
1.09 A for 5CF0 vs 3T0H) and defined each pocket as all residues with an atom within 5.0 A of
the bound ligand (ADP in 3K60, FJS in 5CF0). Residues were matched position by position in 3D.

Findings that drive the design:
  a) The pocket is highly conserved. In 3T0H vs 3K60, 24 of 28 matched positions are identical.
     3T0H and 5CF0 are the same human protein (27 of 27 identical). Affinity can therefore come
     from the shared residues, and selectivity must come from a small number of positions.
  b) Rigid conserved anchor residues (human numbering), which stay put in all structures:
     Asn51, Asp54, Ala55, Asp93, Ile96, Gly97, Met98, Phe138, Tyr139, Trp162, Thr184
     (supported by Leu48, Asp102, Leu103, Gly137). Lys58 is conserved but its side chain moves
     3.1 A between human structures, so it is treated as a flexible anchor.
  c) Four differing positions are rigid and within reach of a ligand (selectivity handles):

     Handle              Difference (human -> 3K60)       Dist. to FJS   Reach     Priority
     Ser52  -> Ala38     loses OH, same size              3.9 A          contact   High
     Val186 -> Ile173    +1 methyl in 3K60 (+27 A^3)      3.4 A          contact   High
     Thr152 -> Ser138    -1 methyl in 3K60 (-27 A^3)      5.1 A          edge      Medium-high
     His154 -> Asn140    loses ring in 3K60 (-39 A^3)     5.9 A          edge      Medium

     Lys112 -> Arg98 is also different but 9.9 A from FJS, so it is not used in round 1.
  d) Two regions move: loop 106-111 (CA-CA shifts of 3 to 9 A, mobile even within the human
     protein) and region 132-136 (positioned differently in 3K60 after register correction).
     These are not used as rigid anchors. They are handled separately (Section 5, Step 6).


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. DESIGN CONCEPT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each ligand is built from three parts:

      [ ANCHOR ]  --  [ LINKER / GROWTH VECTOR ]  --  [ SELECTIVITY HANDLE ]

  ANCHOR   A core that binds the rigid, conserved residues in 2b. It gives potency on both
           proteins and must adopt the same pose in both.
  LINKER   Holds the handle group at the right place and angle. Rigid where possible, to limit
           the entropy lost on binding.
  HANDLE   A small substituent designed against ONE differing residue from 2c. It is built to
           fit the target and to either clash with, or fail to interact with, the residue in
           the off-target.

Selectivity mechanisms, in order of preference:
  1. Steric clash with a rigid off-target residue (most reliable; a rigid side chain cannot move
     away). Used at Val186/Ile173, Thr152/Ser138 and His154/Asn140.
  2. Loss of a specific interaction in the off-target (hydrogen bond to Ser52 OH, which Ala38
     cannot provide).
  3. Conformational selectivity (stabilising a loop state that exists in only one protein).
  4. Combination of 2 or more handles, because their effects add in free energy and multiply
     in affinity. About 1.4 kcal/mol is a 10-fold change, so two handles worth 1 to 2 kcal/mol
     each could give around 100-fold.

The ligand is not required to touch every pocket corner. Potency needs a few strong, rigid
contacts (Asp93 hydrogen bond, Gly97/Met98 backbone, packing against Phe138/Trp162/Ile96).
Extra contacts add size but not selectivity.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. STARTING POINT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FJS (5CF0) already binds the human pocket, and its contact residues and distances are known.
Round 1 grows from FJS toward the handles:
    toward Val186 and Ser52 : short vectors (3.4 to 3.9 A), contact-level substitution
    toward Thr152 and His154: edge growth (5.1 to 5.9 A), needs a 1 to 2 A linker
For ligands favouring the non-human protein, the ADP footprint in 3K60 (and any known fragment
for that protein) is used as the starting point with the same handle logic in reverse.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. METHOD, STEP BY STEP
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1  Structural comparison (completed)
        Output: anchors, handles, reach and rigidity (Section 2).

Step 2  Cavity-volume analysis
        Compute the cavity of each superimposed pocket (POVME, fpocket, CASTp or a grid in
        PyMOL/Python). Take:
          intersection volume    = space present in both pockets -> the anchor must fit here
          target-only volume     = space present only in the target -> the handle must fill it
          off-target-only volume = space present only in the off-target -> never design into it
        Criterion: proceed with a handle only if a target-only volume of about 20 A^3 or more
        lies next to a reachable growth vector.

Step 3  Design enumeration
        At each growth vector from the anchor/FJS, enumerate small substituents chosen by the
        handle type:
          steric handles      : methyl, ethyl, cyclopropyl, halogen, small branched groups
                                (sized to overlap the off-target residue by roughly 0.5 to 1.0 A)
          polar handle (Ser52): hydrogen-bond donors/acceptors (OH, NH2, amide, small heterocycle)
          hydrophobic handle  : methyl, CF3, halogen facing Ala38
          His154 handle       : groups able to hydrogen bond or pi stack with His (human),
                                or bulk toward the Asn140 site (non-human protein)
        Clash handles are placed against rigid residues only. Mobile residues can move away.

Step 4  Docking to an ensemble
        Dock every candidate into 5CF0, 3T0H and 3K60 plus MD snapshots of each, not one frame.
        Criteria: same anchor pose in both proteins, and a clear score or pose advantage in the
        target across most of the ensemble.

Step 5  Water check
        Examine ordered waters near Ser52/Ala38 and Thr152/Ser138 in all structures and assess
        them with a water analysis tool (GIST, WaterMap or 3D-RISM). Hydrogen-bond handles are
        kept only if the water network does not cancel the gain.

Step 6  Conformational selectivity
        Run MD on the apo and bound human protein and on 3K60. Cluster the conformations of
        loop 106-111 and region 132-136, identify states unique to each protein, and test
        whether any candidate stabilises the target-specific state.

Step 7  Molecular dynamics of the complexes
        100 ns or longer, three replicas per ligand-protein complex.
        Criterion: the handle contact (or the clash in the off-target) persists, and the off-
        target does not escape the clash by rearranging its loop.

Step 8  Free energy calculations
        Relative FEP between matched pairs (for example the Ser/Thr and Val/Ile positions) to
        quantify the handle effect.
        Criterion: predicted selectivity of at least 1.5 kcal/mol (about 10-fold) to proceed.

Step 9  Synthesis and testing
        Synthesise the top candidates and measure binding or inhibition against BOTH proteins.
        Selectivity ratio = affinity (off-target) / affinity (target).
        Goal: 30-fold or better, with nanomolar potency.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. DESIGN ROUTES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ROUTE H : ligands selective for the HUMAN protein (off-target = 3K60)
  Anchor  : rigid core on Asp93, Gly97, Met98, Phe138, Tyr139, Thr184, Trp162
  Handle 1: hydrogen-bond group to the Ser52 OH (Ala38 in 3K60 cannot respond)
  Handle 2: small group beside Val186 that clashes with the extra methyl of Ile173
  Optional: group engaging His154 (the residue is Asn140 in 3K60)

ROUTE P : ligands selective for the NON-HUMAN protein (off-target = human)
  Anchor  : same core
  Handle 1: group growing into the room next to Ser138 (clashes with the Thr152 methyl in human)
  Handle 2: bulk toward the Asn140 site (clashes with the His154 ring in human)
  Avoid   : growth toward Ile173, where 3K60 is more crowded

Each route will use the matched-pair control below.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. CONTROLS AND VALIDATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Matched-pair control: for every handle ligand, a second ligand without the handle but with
    the same anchor and linker. If the handle is responsible for selectivity, the control loses
    it. This separates a handle effect from a general scaffold effect.
  - Anchor-only reference: the anchor alone should bind both proteins similarly, showing the
    potency is conserved and the handle supplies the difference.
  - Reverse-direction test: a handle designed for one protein should disfavour the other. The
    two routes in Section 6 cross-check each other.
  - Additional off-targets (paralogues or other family members) will be added to the pocket
    comparison before synthesis, so a handle that beats 3K60 does not lose selectivity against
    another protein.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. EXPECTED OUTPUTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - A ranked list of handles with distance, rigidity, and chemistry rules (Section 2c, 3).
  - Cavity-volume maps: intersection, target-only and off-target-only regions.
  - A set of designed ligands for Route H and/or Route P, each with docking, MD and FEP data.
  - Matched-pair controls for every handle.
  - Measured affinity and selectivity ratios for the synthesised compounds.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. LIMITATIONS AND HOW I WILL ADDRESS THEM
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Crystal structures are single snapshots, and 3T0H is apo.
      -> Use 5CF0 (bound) as the main human reference and MD ensembles for flexibility.
  - Only residues within 5 A of the ligand were compared; second-shell residues and loop
    dynamics are not captured.
      -> MD (Steps 6 and 7) tests whether the handle effect holds in a moving pocket.
  - The distances to handles come from FJS and ADP. New ligands will sit slightly differently.
      -> Re-measure distances on every docked pose.
  - Docking scores cannot resolve about 1 kcal/mol differences.
      -> FEP (Step 8) and experiment (Step 9) make the decision.
  - Ligands for an ATP/ADP site compete with cellular nucleotide.
      -> High potency is required, not selectivity alone.
  - Protonation or tautomer state of His154 can change the His/Asn handle.
      -> Test both states in docking and MD.
